Open with honesty

The first sentence says everything is simulated. Judges trust the rest more when they hear it from you first.

Two beats to remember

The hematocrit false alarm, and the concern Earth rules miss. If a judge remembers only these, the idea has landed.

End on the choice

Rules instead of a prediction model was deliberate. Say why in one breath, then the closing line.

After the video

Questions a judge may ask

Why no machine learning?

No public in-flight data exists to train on, and a crew far from help needs to see why a flag was raised. Rules with sources can be audited. A model’s score cannot.

Is the privacy real?

In a local run, yes: the server refuses requests outside your role. The public demo link has no server, so roles are for show there, and the app says so in a banner.

Is any of the data real?

The lab ranges and pre-flight blood values come from NASA’s open data for the Inspiration4 crew. The radiation rates are measured values. Every day-by-day reading is simulated.

How do you know the thresholds are right?

Each one links to its source. Where we found none, the screen says “Demo parameter”. Several sources were read as abstracts and are marked for a full check.

What about the Moon?

The delay and radiation rate are sourced. The effect of Moon gravity on the body has never been measured over a long stay, so the app applies an assumption and labels it.

What would it take to be real?

Real device data, the agency’s identity system, encryption, and clinical validation of every rule by flight surgeons.
