Earth’s normal ranges fail in two ways

FALSE ALARM

Hematocrit rises 10 to 12% in flight

An Earth lab range calls that dehydration. In space it is expected: plasma volume drops in weightlessness.

Earth: Check

Space: Normal

MISSED

Cabin CO2 that passes Earth limits

It is under the workplace limit, yet crews get headaches. On the station each extra 1 mmHg doubled the odds.

Earth: Normal

Space: Check

MISSED

1.6 litres of urine a day

Normal on Earth. In space, bones shed calcium into the urine, so NASA wants 2 litres to avoid kidney stones.

Earth: Normal

Space: Check

Sources: Kunz 2017, Law 2014, NASA renal stone studies

What you can trust, and what is simulated

REAL

The rules and their sources

Published spaceflight studies and NASA standards behind each rule, linked on screen.

Lab ranges and pre-flight blood values from NASA’s open data.

Radiation rates measured on the station, the Moon and the way to Mars.

SIMULATED

The crew and every reading

Real astronaut vitals are not public, so the readings are generated.

Thresholds we could not source say “Demo parameter” on screen.

Emergency steps marked “demo only”.

BY CHOICE

Rules, not a prediction model

There is no public in-flight data to train on.

It must run on board with no connection.

A crew far from help must see why a flag was raised, and check it.

Every signal links to its NASA Human Research Program risk page
